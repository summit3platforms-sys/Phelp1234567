The Quick Answer

HP printer keeps disconnecting from Wi-Fi? A repair tech explains router settings, sleep modes, and the hidden EWS power setting most guides miss.

It's not your imagination, and it's not a coincidence that it happens most often after the printer's been idle a while. When an HP printer keeps disconnecting from Wi-Fi — showing offline every morning, dropping mid-afternoon, needing a restart to reconnect — there's almost always a specific, fixable reason, and in my repair shop the reason is rarely a weak signal. It's usually one of a short list of settings actively working against a stable connection, on either the router's side or the printer's own power-saving side.

Let's go through the causes in the order I'd check them, ending with a setting that's completely invisible from the printer's own control panel — which is exactly why almost nobody finds it.

Quick-Fix Summary

Fast version:

  1. Move to 2.4GHz if your router splits bands — better range, better compatibility.
  2. Give the printer a fixed spot in your router's DHCP so its address never gets reassigned mid-print.
  3. Check for mesh network roaming issues — printers often struggle when they're supposed to hand off between nodes.
  4. Update the printer's firmware and router's firmware — both, not just one.
  5. Disable the printer's aggressive Wi-Fi sleep mode via its hidden web-based settings page — my uncommon tip.

Fix 1: Get on the 2.4GHz Band

If your router broadcasts both 2.4GHz and 5GHz — as separate network names, or combined under one name with automatic band-switching — connect your printer specifically to the 2.4GHz network.

Why this works: 5GHz is faster but has noticeably shorter range and weaker penetration through walls and floors. A printer sitting in a back office or a kitchen counter, one room away from the router, can hold a 5GHz connection just well enough to seem fine — until normal household interference (a microwave, a neighbor's Wi-Fi, someone walking between it and the router) knocks it loose. 2.4GHz reaches farther and tolerates more interference, at the cost of speed you'll never notice on a print job.

Fix 2: Reserve the Printer's IP Address

In your router's settings (usually reached by typing the router's IP address into a browser — check the label on the router itself, or its app), find the DHCP client list and locate your printer. Set up a DHCP reservation (sometimes called a "static lease") so the printer always receives the exact same local IP address rather than getting a new one assigned periodically.

Why this works: by default, routers hand out addresses on a lease that expires and renews. Most of the time renewal is invisible — but occasionally a printer's address changes while a print job or a monitoring connection still expects the old one, and from your computer's point of view that looks exactly like the printer vanishing off the network. A reservation removes this entire category of disconnect permanently, and it's a five-minute one-time setup.

Fix 3: Watch for Mesh Network Roaming Trouble

If you run a mesh Wi-Fi system (multiple nodes covering a large home), check which node the printer is actually connected to, and see if it's switching between nodes as you or the system trigger a "roam." Some mesh systems aggressively push connected devices toward whichever node currently has the strongest signal — great for phones that move around the house, often disastrous for a printer that sits in exactly one spot and doesn't need to roam at all.

Why this works: printers generally have simpler Wi-Fi radios than phones or laptops, and many handle a forced hand-off between mesh nodes poorly — dropping the connection entirely rather than smoothly transferring it. If your mesh system's app allows you to pin a device to a specific node, or exclude certain devices from aggressive roaming, do that for the printer. It removes the printer from a negotiation it was never built to handle gracefully.

Fix 4: Update Both Firmwares

Check for and install the latest firmware on the printer itself (usually through its control panel's network or update menu, or via the HP Smart app) and on your router separately, through the router manufacturer's app or web interface.

Why this works: Wi-Fi compatibility bugs get discovered and patched on both sides of a connection over a device's lifetime. A printer firmware from its release year and a router firmware from years later can develop small incompatibilities neither manufacturer anticipated at launch — patched individually on each side as issues surface, but only if you actually install the updates. This is boring, unglamorous maintenance, and it quietly resolves more connectivity oddities than people expect.

Fix 5: Disable Wi-Fi Sleep Through the Printer's Hidden Web Page (My Uncommon Tip)

Here's the fix for the specific pattern that fools almost everyone: the printer works perfectly all day, then shows offline first thing every morning, or after any long idle stretch — but reconnects fine the moment you walk over and press a button on it.

Most HP printers include an aggressive power-saving feature that puts the Wi-Fi radio into a deep sleep state after extended inactivity, to save a small amount of energy. On many models, this setting is either buried several menus deep on the control panel or not exposed on the control panel at all — it only lives in the printer's Embedded Web Server (EWS), a built-in configuration webpage most owners never know exists.

To reach it: find your printer's IP address (usually under its network settings menu, or by printing a network configuration page), then type that IP address directly into a web browser on any computer on the same network. This opens the EWS — a full settings dashboard living inside the printer itself. Look under Power Management, Network, or Sleep Settings for something labeled Wireless Sleep, Wi-Fi Auto-Off, or similar, and set it to disabled, or extend the delay to its maximum.

Why this works: deep Wi-Fi sleep doesn't just dim a screen — it powers the radio down enough that reconnecting cleanly isn't guaranteed, and some printers wake sluggishly or fail to rejoin the network automatically at all, requiring a manual nudge to come back. The EWS exists specifically because printers have more configuration options than a simple front panel can display, and power management is exactly the kind of setting HP tucks away there rather than surfacing on the touchscreen. In my shop, this single toggle has ended more "offline every morning" service calls than any router setting on this list — because the printer, not the network, turned its own radio off on purpose.

When to Call a Professional

If you've moved to 2.4GHz, reserved the IP address, ruled out mesh roaming issues, updated both firmwares, and disabled Wi-Fi sleep through the EWS — and disconnections still happen randomly throughout active use, not just after idle periods — a failing Wi-Fi radio inside the printer becomes the more likely explanation. Contact HP support with your model and serial number; wireless module faults are a standard warranty repair category. Out of warranty, a wired Ethernet or USB connection is often the more practical fix on an older printer than a wireless module replacement, and it's worth mentioning as a permanent workaround while you decide.

FAQ

Why does my HP printer go offline every morning specifically?

That pattern points to the printer's own Wi-Fi sleep setting, not your router. After a long idle stretch overnight, the radio powers down aggressively and doesn't always reconnect cleanly. Disable Wireless Sleep in the printer's Embedded Web Server (EWS).

What is the printer's Embedded Web Server and how do I find it?

It's a settings dashboard built into the printer, reached by typing the printer's IP address into any browser on the same network. Find the IP under the printer's network settings menu, or on a printed network configuration page.

Should I use 2.4GHz or 5GHz for my printer?

2.4GHz, generally — it has better range and tolerates interference and distance better than 5GHz, and a print job doesn't need 5GHz's extra speed.

Can my mesh Wi-Fi system really cause printer disconnects?

Yes. Some mesh systems aggressively hand devices off between nodes to chase the strongest signal, and printers often handle that hand-off poorly. If your mesh app allows pinning a device to one node, use it for the printer.

An HP printer that keeps disconnecting from Wi-Fi is almost always fighting one specific setting, not a weak signal in general. Get it on 2.4GHz, reserve its IP address, watch for mesh roaming trouble, keep both firmwares current, and check that hidden EWS Wi-Fi sleep setting most owners never knew existed. That last one alone solves the "fine all day, offline every morning" pattern more often than everything else on this list combined.
